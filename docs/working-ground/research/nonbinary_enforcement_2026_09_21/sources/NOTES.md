## Exact closest result: the inherited strategic theorem

The closest theorem is R13's `math/AUDIT.md`, §§2–5, with arbitrary positive
report costs. Its game at receiver fine zero and effective costs k_i+e is
identical, terminal payoff by terminal payoff, to R20's committed report
sanction. This is a **direct matching-assumption implication**, not merely an
analogy. It provides all mixed-continuation and equilibrium-consistency
arguments. The new formula is obtained by solving the weak blocker inequality
for e. No original-equilibrium-theorem claim is appropriate.

R15 `math/RESULTS.md`, Theorems A/B and the exact-interface argument, are the
closest sources for contextual distinguishability and composition. R20 derives
downward-interface preservation by monotonicity and contextual necessity from
the zero slice. Its quantitative bound is a threshold shift using R15's
directed margin bound. Reuse and finite-label transport are corresponding
corollaries of R15/R16, not a new tensorization result.

## External disclosure-cost comparison

Kartik, Lee and Suen, *Multi-Sender Disclosure with Costs*, January 2026,
[author-hosted paper](https://navinkartik.com/Papers/html/KLS-disclosure-costs/),
§2 and Propositions 1–2, explicitly model a utility cost of furnishing evidence
and characterize single-sender threshold equilibria and disclosure-cost
comparative statics. The model, these propositions and the introduction were
inspected. Their senders have linear preferences over a receiver's posterior;
private signals concern a shared payoff state, and their main timing is
simultaneous (with a sequential extension discussed). R20 instead has independent
complementary bits, receiver action rewards, a prescribed sequential order and
the all-silent target. Those propositions do not directly yield R20's suffix
formula under matching assumptions. Nevertheless, interpreting an observable
report charge as an increased disclosure cost is standard, not an original
modelling discovery. No claim is made that this paper excludes an equivalent
special case elsewhere in the disclosure literature.

Kreps and Wilson's [Sequential Equilibria](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/sequential-equilibrium)
supplies the standard assessment concept; R13/R14 record the inspected definition
and belief proof. R20 borrows it unchanged. Commitment to automatic collections
is a declared primitive. No theorem about an endogenous enforcer or institutional
feasibility is borrowed or asserted.
