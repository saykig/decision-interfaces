**Result in plain English.** Knowing the range of possible rewards and the range
of detection probabilities separately can give the wrong minimum fine. We must
retain which rewards occur with which detection probabilities. For the declared
one-sender model, an exact piecewise-linear curve keeps precisely the information
needed for every permitted fine question. It survives independent extra inspection
stages and exact finite compatibility masks. It does not survive arbitrary new
reward attachments: an explicit second counterexample identifies that boundary.

## 1. Model and operations, before the formula

Keep R21's one-sender hard-evidence game. Nature draws a private bit with fixed
probability p=1/2. Type zero can only be silent S; type one can send an
authenticated report R at known fixed cost k>0. The receiver observes the message
and chooses C or D, with payoffs 0 under C, 1 under D when the bit is one, and
-2 under D otherwise. A reporting type receives reward a*r if D is chosen, where
a>0 is a public query parameter. A silent sender receives that reward only if D
is chosen, as in the inherited game; the receiver will not choose D after S.

After the receiver acts, a Bernoulli audit detects a report with probability q.
The institution commits before play to a fine e>=0 collected from the sender
only on detection of an actual report. Collection is authenticated and automatic;
there is no optimizing inspector or budget constraint. The audit has no false
positives on silence. Its realized draw is unavailable before either decision.
Conditional on the actual parameters, it is independent of the private bit.

Players know actual r,q,a,k,e. The analyst knows only a nonempty compact family
K contained in `(0,infinity) x (0,1)` of possible pairs (r,q). Joint uncertainty
is epistemic dependence between parameters, not an assertion that two physical
random draws are correlated. K may be a curve generated by a single latent
parameter; convex polytopes are the exact executable class. Query a,k are fixed
across all pairs. The institution must use one fine e for every pair, with
existential favorable ties. No all-equilibria or welfare claim is made.

Allowed reusable operations in this phase: change positive a,k; connect independent
serial audit stages; repeat those stages as fresh independent occurrences; take
finite labelled alternatives subject to an explicit mask. A reward shift making
the effective report cost negative is NOT an allowed operation of the base profile.

## 2. T1: primitive equilibrium and the attained frontier

For any behavioral report probability s in [0,1], the posterior after S is
`p(1-s)/(1-p*s) <= 1/2`. The receiver strictly chooses C there. After an
authenticated report the bit is one, so it strictly chooses D. The denominator
is at least 1/2, and this posterior extends continuously to off-path reports;
fully mixed feasible trembles supply the inherited consistent beliefs.

The reporting type's payoff, by enumerating the two audit leaves, is

    q*(a*r-k-e) + (1-q)*(a*r-k) = a*r-k-e*q.

Silence gives zero. Every mixture is an affine combination of these two payoffs.
Thus a silent sequential-equilibrium target exists exactly when
`a*r-k-e*q <= 0`. At equality silence is allowed but not forced. A positive gain
excludes the target; mixing cannot rescue it. The receiver's choice was derived
before this comparison, not built into an assumed equilibrium definition.

Consequently

    E_K(a,k) = max(0, max_{(r,q) in K} (a*r-k)/q).             (T1)

Compactness and strict positivity of q give a positive floor and an attained
maximum. At E every model's primitive incentive is nonpositive. Any nonnegative
fine below E fails at a maximizing model whenever E>0. This proves attainment
and minimality, not just a numerical threshold.

**Evidence:** written elementary proof; exact finite primitive payoff and
mixed-best-reply replays. **LEAN: YES as a future stable bridge; NO new Lean
proof has been executed in R22.** The expected-penalty calculation is standard,
not an originality claim.

## 3. T2: equal marginal information, different decisions

Let

    K_A = conv{(1,1/4), (2,1/2)},
    K_B = conv{(1,1/2), (2,1/4)}.

Both reward projections are the entire interval [1,2], and both detection
projections are the entire interval [1/4,1/2]. Thus they have identical complete
marginal uncertainty sets, not merely the same means. Take a=1,k=1/2. T3 below
justifies checking vertices over the entire segments. Their minima are

    E_A = max(2,3) = 3,        E_B = max(1,6) = 6.

At fine 3 the second B endpoint has gain `2-1/2-3/4 = 3/4 > 0`.
At fine 6 its gain is zero. A's high reward is paired with better detection;
B's high reward is paired with worse detection. The marginal rectangle gives
6 and is a conservative outer bound for A, but is not A's exact answer.

Both sources are one-dimensional affine families. One shared scalar therefore
suffices to generate the failure. We do not claim universal game-theoretic
minimality. The example does not refute R21's fixed-reward theorem: it violates
that theorem's fixed-reward premise. Nor does it refute R15's declared disclosure
query class. Increasing precision of an exactly equal marginal summary cannot
restore discarded dependence.

**Evidence:** complete rational counterexample and primitive threshold replay.
**LEAN: NO for this isolated example; prioritize the general T1/T3 bridge.**

## 4. T3: a sufficient and necessary operational curve

Define the ratio transformation and restricted support profile

    T(r,q) = (x,y) = (r/q,1/q),
    J_K(t) = max(0, max_{(x,y) in T(K)} (x-t*y)),  t>0.

Equivalently J is the support of `conv(T(K) union {(0,0)})` evaluated only in
directions (1,-t). Positive clipping is essential: a negative unconstrained
fine is not an available decision. Only the exposed boundary in these directions
is needed, not the full transformed set or the full joint source.

**Sufficiency.** T1 gives `E_K(a,k)=a*J_K(k/a)` for all a,k>0.

**Necessity.** When J_K(t) differs from J_L(t) for some t>0, the permitted query
a=1,k=t has different attained minima. Thus equality of J on t>0 is exactly
equality of the complete declared fine-answer function. For rational-only queries,
the same equivalence holds: compactness and positive q make J continuous, so
any strict difference persists on an interval containing a positive rational t.
This is the coarsest operational equivalence, not a claim that the profile is
already an optimal finite-bit code. It is also more than naming a supremum:
T1 derived it from the game, T2 demonstrated missing dependence, and the following
construction makes the quotient finite and executable for rational polytopes.

**Convexity/vertex reduction.** Suppose `K=conv{(r_i,q_i)}` and
`(r,q)=sum_i lambda_i*(r_i,q_i)`. Let `mu_i=lambda_i*q_i/q`. Then mu is a convex
weight vector and

    (r/q,1/q) = sum_i mu_i*(r_i/q_i,1/q_i).

Conversely, for convex mu on transformed vertices, put
`lambda_i=(mu_i/q_i)/(sum_j mu_j/q_j)` to recover a source convex combination.
Hence `T(K)=conv{T(r_i,q_i)}`. In particular

    J_K(t) = max(0, max_i (r_i/q_i-t/q_i)).                    (T3)

Unlike R15's weighted logs, no interior source point can improve this
linear-fractional maximum beyond all source vertices. This is a property of
this different objective, not permission to use vertex-only queries in R18.

The curve is continuous, convex, nonincreasing and zero for t at or above the
largest reward. Its nonzero pieces are exposed affine functions of transformed
vertices. Exact rational line intersections and clipping give a canonical
piecewise-affine representation. The reference algorithm enumerates O(m^2)
candidate intersections, selects the largest line on each interval, then merges
identical neighboring pieces: O(m^3) rational comparisons in this simple
implementation, with polynomial bit work. A point query is O(m) rational
operations. No optimal runtime, compression rate, or multi-sender algorithm is
claimed. Duplicate vertices, equal slopes, dominated lines and exact ties are
covered. t=0 is the right-hand limit, included only for representation/replay.

For example, adding (1,1/2) to the singleton source {(2,1/4)} changes the joint
hull but not J: the added line is everywhere irrelevant on t>=0. This disproves
the unnecessarily strong assertion that the entire joint hull must be retained.

**Evidence:** written proof, rational transformation identities and exact envelope
replays. **LEAN: YES for T1-to-profile equivalence and the convex-weight identity;
formalization remains open.** The projective/linear-fractional geometry is established
mathematics; the role here is an explicit operational interface specialization.

## 5. T4: approximation, conditioning and exact discrepancy

Let P,Q be compact sources. Suppose that for every (r,q) in P there is
(r',q') in Q with `r<=r'+eps_r`, `q>=q'-eps_q`, where eps_r,eps_q>=0.
Suppose all P detection probabilities are at least q0>0. Set E_Q=E_Q(a,k).
At the matched point in Q, `a*r'-k<=E_Q*q'`. Therefore

    a*r-k <= E_Q*q + a*eps_r + E_Q*eps_q.

Increasing the fine by `(a*eps_r+E_Q*eps_q)/q0` makes every P incentive
nonpositive. Thus the directed operational bound is

    E_P <= E_Q + (a*eps_r+E_Q*eps_q)/q0.                     (T4)

This statement needs matching joint points; separate marginal approximations do
not imply its premise. The coefficient is sharp: take the singleton sources
P={(r+eps_r,q0)}, Q={(r,q0+eps_q)} with `a*r>k` and valid positive probabilities.
Substituting their T1 values gives equality, including either zero error coordinate.
A symmetric bound follows by applying the directed result in both directions,
with the corresponding floors and frontier values.

No uniform absolute sensitivity bound survives as detection approaches zero.
With r=a=1,k=1/2 and q=1/m versus 2/m, detection errors are 1/m while the fine
difference is m/4. R21's detection-floor premise remains operationally important.

For interval-limited profile approximation, define
`d_[u,v](P,Q)=max_{u<=t<=v}|J_P(t)-J_Q(t)|`, `0<=u<=v<infinity`.
For polytopes this discrepancy is exactly attained at an endpoint or a breakpoint
of either envelope: between breakpoints the difference is affine and its absolute
value has its maximum at an endpoint. The implementation computes this exact
quantity, not a sample estimate. An error bound delta gives fine error at most
`a*delta` whenever `k/a` lies in the declared interval. This does not license
queries outside the interval. No new entropy or bit-rate theorem is asserted.

**Evidence:** written inequalities and sharp equality examples; exact finite
replays. **LEAN: YES for the directed inequality; not yet formalized.**

## 6. T5: independent serial composition and finite masks

Attach an independent audit stage with detection s in compact V subset (0,1),
requiring both detections for collection. Independence means both conditional
lotteries are independent and the uncertainty family is K x V. Detection becomes
q*s. Maximizing the positive part of `(a*r-k)/(q*s)` gives

    E_(K x V) = E_K / min V.

For fresh independent serial stages, divide by the product of all detection
floors. Repeating the same encoded stage k times divides by its floor k times;
this is not reuse of the same realized lottery or an unrecorded shared parameter.
If the log error of each positive floor is delta_i, the frontier multiplicative
factor is at most `exp(sum_i delta_i)` when E_K>0. Exact zero remains zero.
Profile absolute error delta is amplified to `a*delta/product_i floor_i`.

For exact finite labels, let K_l and V_m be the within-branch independent
families and A the permitted nonempty set of pairs. The global answer is

    max_{(l,m) in A} E_(K_l)/min(V_m).

An empty mask means no admissible model, represented as None, not as a feasible
zero-fine result. Marginal maximization can be wrong: with K_low={(1,1/2)},
K_high={(2,1/2)}, floors 1/4 and 3/4, and only matching low/low or high/high pairs,
a=1,k=1/2 gives max(4,4)=4. Allowing the forbidden high/low pair gives 12.

**Evidence:** written maximization proof and exact product/repetition/mask replays.
**LEAN: YES for stable composition/mask semantics, not yet formalized.** No OR-audit,
correlated physical-audit, general continuous shared-parameter or tree optimizer
is supplied by this API.

## 7. T6: a new operation can break even the repaired profile

It would be false to call J sufficient for arbitrary future reward composition.
Take K={(2,1/2)} and L=conv{(2,1/2),(1/4,1/8)}. On t>=0 the extra line
`2-8*t` never exceeds `max(0,4-2*t)`, so J_K=J_L for every permitted query.
Now add a known background reward b=4, with a=1 and k=1. The effective t is -3,
outside the declared profile's domain. The fine minima become 10 and 26.

This is not an implementation error; the interface deliberately discarded the
negative-t directions. A reward-shift extension would require the appropriately
expanded directional profile or source recovery. The checker rejects a direct
negative-t query rather than silently extrapolating its truncated envelope.
We exhibit this boundary without adding bargaining, dynamics or vector sanctions.

**Evidence:** written all-positive-t equality and exact rational separation.
**LEAN: NO for the isolated counterexample.**
